IRS § 162 vs. § 262 Deductibility Rules

100% Business Tools: Software solely used for work (Canva Pro, AWS, Zoom, Google Workspace, GitHub) qualifies as a full ordinary & necessary expense under IRC § 162(a) (Schedule C Line 18 or 25).

Mixed Personal & Work: Utilities such as home internet, mobile phones, or Amazon Prime qualify for a reasonable split (e.g., 50% business / 50% personal under IRC § 262).

Personal Entertainment: Netflix, Spotify, Disney+, and gaming cannot be written off as business deductions.

Deceptive Retention & Dark Patterns

The Pause Trap: Services like Canva and Audible promote "Pause for 3 Months" while burying cancellation. When 90 days elapse, billing silently resumes without notification.

Early Termination Penalties: Adobe contracts often levy a 50% remainder fee ($100–$300). Bypass: Switching to a cheaper plan resets the statutory 14-day cooling-off period, allowing cancellation with $0 penalty.

Ghost Seat Invoices: Deleting an employee in Google Workspace or Slack does not cancel their paid seat license until explicitly downgraded.

Cross-Border Currency & Credit Card Spreads

Over 90% of global software is billed in US Dollars ($). When charged to non-US credit cards, banks silently tack on a 2.5% to 4.0% foreign transaction spread on every recurring invoice.
