01. THE DUAL-USE TAX TRAP

IRS § 162 vs. § 262 Allocation

Everyday professionals either write off $0 (losing \$500–\$2,000 in legal refunds because software was charged to a personal card) or claim 100% of everything (including personal streaming or home internet, inviting an automated audit penalty).

The Rule: Dedicated work tools (Canva, Zoom, AWS, domains, Google Workspace) are 100% tax-deductible. Dual-use utilities (phone, cloud storage, broadband) require an honest proportional split (e.g. 50% business). Our tool handles this calculation automatically.
02. THE "PAUSED" RETENTION TRAP

Deceptive Cancellation Dark Patterns

Clicking "Cancel" often does not actually cancel. Services like Canva, Adobe, and Audible offer a deceptive "Pause for 3 Months" button. You feel relief, but 90 days later billing quietly resumes without an alert.

The Rule: True cancellation requires revoking the underlying recurring mandate. Furthermore, in tools like Google Workspace and Slack, deleting a departed teammate's email does not cancel their license seat — you keep paying \$18/mo for an empty ghost seat forever until you explicitly downgrade your seat count.

The Rule: As local currencies fluctuate against the US dollar, your recurring software quietly increases in domestic cost every year, even when the software vendor never announced a price hike.
